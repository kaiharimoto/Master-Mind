# demo-script.md — the two-minute arc

For a technical audience: peers, a portfolio reel, a post. Two surfaces on one
screen — a Windows window and an Android device profile — both live on the same
map. Nothing is pre-baked; every beat below is a thing the tool does.

The spine of the demo is the defining story: **space is the memory.** Everything
else is in service of getting someone back to where a thought was.

---

## 0:00 – 0:12 · Open on the whole brain

Windows, mind expansion. The 150-node fermentation notebook, three years of it,
on screen at once. Districts read by colour: microbiology, lacto-vegetables,
sourdough, koji, vinegar, dairy, safety, equipment, reading — and a small coral
cluster off to the upper right that is only three weeks old.

> "This is three years of one person's notes. You can see the shape of it. The
> old dense neighbourhoods, and that bright corner — that's the frontier, added
> this month."

Point at the dashed ring low in the frame: eight thoughts still waiting.

**Artifacts 04, 06.**

## 0:12 – 0:30 · Capture is one action; placement is deliberate

Switch to canvas. Type a thought into the capture field, press Enter. It lands
in the holding cluster in the unplaced state — dashed ring, slow pulse — and the
holding count ticks up.

> "Capture never asks me where it goes. It goes to holding. That's the whole
> point: I don't want to make a filing decision while I'm having the thought."

Then drag it out to a permanent spot, next to the district it belongs with. It
stays exactly where it was dropped. The holding count ticks down.

**Artifacts 19, 08.**

## 0:30 – 0:48 · Refinding is the real feature

Type "grape leaf" into search. The view flies — not to a list, to the node,
where it actually lives, in the middle of the lacto-vegetables district, with
its neighbours around it and the search-hit marks lit.

> "I didn't remember the note. I remembered roughly where it was. That's what
> the space is for."

**Artifacts 18, 10.**

## 0:48 – 1:12 · The Windows landmark: hands

Turn hand tracking on in the top bar. The webcam panel appears with the live
landmark skeleton drawn over the hand. Run the vocabulary:

| Pose | What it does |
|---|---|
| open palm, fingers fanned | spread the map |
| fingers together | gather it back in |
| closed fist | grab a district and move it — the cluster keeps its internal arrangement |
| two fingers | select what the hand is over |

Then switch tracking **off** and do the same four operations with the mouse
buttons beside the lens tag.

> "Every hand gesture has a mouse equivalent, because I'm not going to stand in
> front of a room betting the demo on my webcam."

**Artifacts 05, 17, 15.**

## 1:12 – 1:34 · The Android landmark: AR, and the same map

Move to the Android surface, AR lens. The same map, the same positions, oriented
by the gyroscope — the heading and tilt readout is live in the corner. Look
around. Tap a node to inspect it. Search and fly to it from here too.

Then make an edit on Android — rename a node, change its colour — and let the
audience watch it land on the Windows window beside it, positions untouched.

> "One model. Three lenses. The positions never move unless I move them."

**Artifacts 03, 16, 11, 12.**

## 1:34 – 2:00 · The finder, and the safety of it

Open the finder. Generate the prompt — it carries the map's JSON, positions and
all. Paste it into any AI chat. Paste the reply back.

Paste a *bad* reply first, on purpose: a visible error, and the map does not
move. Then the real one: suggestions stage one at a time — a connection, a
grouping, a placement for something still in holding. Accept one; it applies
exactly as described. Reject one; it leaves nothing behind.

> "It never touches the map on its own. It proposes; I decide. And when the
> model returns garbage — which it will — the failure is visible and inert."

**Artifacts 13, 14, 20.**

---

## If something breaks live

- **Hand tracking misbehaves** — switch it off. The four buttons do the same
  four operations.
- **The AR surface is slow** — the canvas lens shows the same map and the same
  positions; the story does not depend on the AR frame rate.
- **The finder reply is malformed** — that is a beat in the demo, not a failure.
